## A. Bottom Line Up Front (BLUF)

**1. [Battlefield]** Russian forces conducted 291 combat engagements on May 30, continuing a record-pace drone and missile bombardment campaign. Russia struck Zaporizhzhia twice in a single day and hit fuel stations in Sumy via drone. Since April 21, Russia has lost a net 69 square miles of occupied territory—a notable reversal—but attrition warfare remains at maximum intensity across all fronts.

> Link: https://empr.media/news/war/russia-ukraine-war-updates-key-developments-as-of-may-30-2026/
> **Confidence: HIGH**

**2. [Defense Articles / Air Defense]** Ukraine's Patriot and NASAMS launchers are at critical interceptor levels — some units holding as few as 5–10 missiles. Secretary Hegseth stated May 30 the U.S. "will find a way" to help following an urgent letter from President Zelensky warning of Ukraine's worsening air defense shortage. No specific transfer announced.
> Source: Kyiv Independent, "Hegseth says US will 'find a way' to help Ukraine after Zelensky's plea for air defense missiles."
> Link: https://kyivindependent.com/hegseth-letter/
> Also: RBC Ukraine, https://newsukraine.rbc.ua/news/ukraine-warns-patriot-and-nasams-launchers-1778198714.html
> **Confidence: HIGH**

**3. [Stockpiles / Epic Fury Linkage]** Operation Epic Fury (Feb 28–May 5, 2026) consumed over 1,600 Patriot PAC-2/3 interceptors in its first five weeks — exceeding the entire annual U.S. production capacity. Replenishment will take 3–5 years. This directly reduced the interceptors available to Ukraine. Both theaters are drawing from the same finite inventory.
> Source: FPRI, "Scaling Patriot Production: The Industrial Base Crisis Explained," May 2026.
> Link: https://www.fpri.org/article/2026/05/scaling-patriot-production-the-industrial-base-crisis-explained/
> Also: RUSI via Pravda EN, https://news-pravda.com/world/2026/03/01/2111417.html
> **Confidence: HIGH**

**4. [Funding]** The FY2026 NDAA provides $400M in Ukraine Security Assistance Initiative (USAI) funding. However, the Pentagon Comptroller described it as "released to be put under contract" — not yet obligated. The House defense appropriations bill includes no Ukraine-specific aid beyond NDAA; the Senate bill includes $800M. A conference will determine the final amount.
> Source: OSW Centre for Eastern Studies, "US Defence Budget 2026," Dec 19, 2025.
> Link: https://www.osw.waw.pl/en/publikacje/analyses/2025-12-19/us-defence-budget-2026-congress-approves-continued-support-ukraine

> **Confidence: HIGH**

**5. [CRINK Supply Chain — Unconfirmed Claim]** Ukrainian President Zelensky has publicly claimed Russia is now supplying Iran with Russian-manufactured Shahed drones for use against U.S. and Israeli forces — a reversal of the earlier supply flow. This claim has not been independently confirmed by U.S. government sources.
> Source: CSIS, "CRINK Security Ties: Growing Cooperation," 2026.

## B. Top Issues for OUSW Comptroller P/B Analysts

**1. [Defense Articles / Stockpiles / Readiness]**
Ukraine's Patriot interceptor shortfall is an active crisis, not a future risk. Launchers at 5–10 missiles. Operation Epic Fury consumed 1,600+ interceptors in 5 weeks; current annual production is 600–650. The 3–5 year replenishment gap is confirmed. Congress will ask whether U.S. homeland and NATO defense readiness is compromised.
*Potential RFI Risk: HIGH*

**2. [USAI / Funding / Appropriations]**
$400M USAI is authorized but not yet under contract. Trump administration has not requested additional Ukraine aid in the base budget. House: $0 Ukraine-specific; Senate: $800M. Conference outcome is unresolved. Analysts should be ready to characterize execution status, what is obligated, and what supplemental or reprogramming action may be needed.
*Potential RFI Risk: HIGH*

**3. [PDA]**
Presidential Drawdown Authority status is unclear. U.S. military aid to Ukraine fell 99% in 2025 under the Trump administration. Any new PDA package will require senior-level authorization. Analysts should be prepared to characterize remaining PDA authority and characterize any political constraints.
*Potential RFI Risk: MEDIUM-HIGH*

**4. [Epic Fury / Iran Linkage / Replenishment]**
The $25B cost of Operation Epic Fury created direct, confirmed competition for the same U.S. air defense interceptors, precision-guided munitions, and sustainment resources needed for Ukraine. CENTCOM demand may continue to take priority over EUCOM requirements even post-ceasefire. Analysts should be ready to explain the cross-theater demand signal and which accounts are most constrained.
*Potential RFI Risk: HIGH*

**5. [Congressional RFI / Oversight]**
Sen. McConnell's public op-ed criticizing DoD for slow Ukraine aid release and bipartisan lawmakers expressing support during a Kyiv visit (May 28) indicate active congressional oversight of Ukraine aid execution. Analysts should be ready to explain what has been released, obligated, and delivered vs. what is stalled.
*Potential RFI Risk: HIGH*

**6. [DIB / Replenishment]**
Pentagon and Lockheed Martin signed a 7-year framework in Jan 2026 to scale PAC-3 MSE production from 600 to 2,000/year by 2030. This is a 4-year ramp; the gap is real and binding. Rocket motors, propellant, and guidance electronics are the key production constraints. Congress may ask whether multi-year procurement (MYP) authorities are fully utilized.
*Potential RFI Risk: MEDIUM*

**7. [Sanctions / CRINK]**
The Russia-Iran-China-North Korea supply chain continues post-Epic Fury. China continues dual-use component supply to Russia. NK troops remain deployed. Russia's Shahed-assembly lines are now ~90% domestic. Analysts must be ready to characterize whether current sanctions are effectively constraining Russia's military production.
*Potential RFI Risk: MEDIUM*

### D. Defense Article Analysis — Required Questions

**What defense article is involved?** Patriot PAC-3 MSE interceptors (primary); NASAMS interceptors (secondary).

**Is it being transferred, requested, depleted, delayed, replenished, or produced?** All of the above simultaneously: being requested by Ukraine, depleted in both Ukraine and post-Epic Fury CENTCOM theater, delayed in replenishment, and being slowly produced with acceleration underway.

**Impact on Ukraine's battlefield position:** Without Patriot resupply, Ukraine cannot protect critical cities and infrastructure from Russian ballistic missile and hypersonic attacks. The Oreshnik deployment against an interceptor-depleted defense is a direct operational consequence.

**Impact on U.S. readiness:** 3–5 year replenishment gap creates a known window of elevated risk for U.S. and NATO collective defense obligations. INDOPACOM pre-positioning requirements are also affected.

**Congressional interest:** HIGH. This is the single most likely topic for HASC/SASC/HAC/SAC hearings on Ukraine in June 2026.

**Appropriations implication:** $4.76B acceleration contract underway. Potential supplemental request for further acceleration or drawdown replenishment not yet publicly announced.

## 11. Analyst Assessment

### What Matters Most Today

The intersection of three simultaneous crises defines the current picture. Ukraine's Patriot and NASAMS interceptor shortage is not a future risk — it is a present operational reality, with launchers holding 5–10 missiles each. Operation Epic Fury's five-week campaign against Iran directly consumed more interceptors than the entire U.S. annual production capacity. And the U.S. defense industrial base is structurally incapable of rapidly closing the gap — the most optimistic scenario puts full replenishment at 2030 at the earliest.

Russia is exploiting this window. Record drone campaigns (8,000+/month) and the first live deployment of the Oreshnik hypersonic against Kyiv signal that Russia recognizes and is actively capitalizing on Ukraine's air defense vulnerability. The tactical situation is deteriorating in direct proportion to the interceptor deficit.

### What Analysts Should Watch Over the Next 7 Days

1. Whether Hegseth's May 30 pledge becomes a specific, announced PDA or transfer
2. House-Senate defense appropriations conference — whether the House $0 or Senate $800M position prevails
3. Any U.S. government confirmation or denial of Zelensky's Russia-to-Iran Shahed supply claim
4. Any Russian follow-up Oreshnik deployment against Ukrainian cities
5. Whether DoD publicly addresses the Epic Fury-Ukraine interceptor competition

### What Could Become a Congressional Issue

The question of whether Operation Epic Fury directly caused Ukraine's interceptor shortage is the most likely immediate oversight flashpoint. The arithmetic is undeniable: 1,600+ interceptors consumed in 5 weeks vs. 600 produced per year vs. Ukraine at 5–10 per launcher. A congressional hearing connecting these three data points is a foreseeable near-term development.

### What Could Affect Budget Execution

USAI funds that are "released but not under contract" represent an execution risk. If June 2026 passes without meaningful obligation, this will generate pressure for reprogramming, supplemental requests, or senior leadership intervention. The $4.76B Patriot acceleration contract is underway but additional funding may be needed.
