## B. TOP ISSUES FOR OUSW COMPTROLLER P/B ANALYSTS

1. **[Stockpiles / Readiness / Epic Fury Linkage]** The U.S. has fewer than 850 Patriot PAC-3 interceptors worldwide following Epic Fury. This creates a direct readiness gap for EUCOM, INDOPACOM, and homeland defense. Ukraine aid has effectively halted for this system. Congress is likely to ask how the Pentagon plans to manage simultaneous demand across CENTCOM post-conflict reconstitution, EUCOM deterrence, NATO reassurance, and potential future crises.

2. **[PDA / Execution]** With PDA largely suspended and approximately $4.96 billion in prior drawdown authority unused, the OUSW P/B office should be prepared to explain the execution gap between authorized authority and actual delivery. A key RFI risk: "Why is unused authority sitting if Ukraine needs weapons?"

3. **[USAI / Appropriations]** The USAI ($33.5B procured since 2022, $3.7B FMF provided) is the last major functioning pipeline. Monthly USAI delivery fell to ~$265M in 2026. The FY2026 Pentagon budget request proposed eliminating Ukraine aid, creating a political and programmatic fault line with Congress. Congress authorized $400M/year for FY2026–FY2027 — a floor, not a ceiling — but execution has not matched even that level.

4. **[Replenishment / DIB]** FY2027 Pentagon munitions request is approximately $70 billion — nearly three times current levels — driven by Epic Fury and Ukraine depletion. P/B analysts should be aware of this budget development and prepared to explain how FY2027 replenishment will be apportioned between CENTCOM, EUCOM, and INDOPACOM requirements.

5. **[Defense Articles / Congressional RFI]** Ukraine is developing an indigenous FP-7.x interceptor and reviving S-300 stockpiles as substitutes for Patriot, but these are not equivalent systems for ballistic missile defense. Congress may ask whether Ukraine's air defense capability will materially degrade over the next 6–12 months and what the U.S. plan is.

6. **[Congressional Interest]** North Korean ballistic missile use on Ukrainian soil on August 11 is a visible, politically charged development. Congress is likely to ask: what is the United States doing to counter North Korean missile transfers to Russia, and are existing sanctions authorities sufficient?

7. **[Epic Fury / Budget]** Russia provided real-time intelligence to Iran during Epic Fury, enabling more precise missile strikes against U.S. forces. This Russia-Iran cooperation is now documented in official and credible reporting. Analysts should anticipate questions about whether this changes the calculus for resumed Ukraine aid as a means of pressuring Russia.
