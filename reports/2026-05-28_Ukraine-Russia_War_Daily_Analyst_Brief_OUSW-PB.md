## A. BOTTOM LINE UP FRONT

Five developments define the environment today:

**1. [Stockpiles/Epic Fury] U.S. munitions inventories are critically depleted across two theaters and cannot be reconstituted for 1–4 years under current production rates.**
Russia launched 163 drones toward Ukraine overnight (May 27), while the U.S. simultaneously faces post-Epic Fury inventory crises: 1,000+ Tomahawks expended (~1/3 of prewar inventory), 1,060–1,430 Patriots used (~46–61% of total stock), and nearly half of THAAD interceptors consumed. Ukraine remains on "starvation rations" for air defense interceptors.
- Source: CSIS, "Last Rounds? Status of Key Munitions at the Iran War Ceasefire," May 2026 — https://www.csis.org/analysis/last-rounds-status-key-munitions-iran-war-ceasefire
- Source: NATO News Pravda, "Ukraine Is Entering an Acute Phase of Shortage of Air Defense Missiles," May 26, 2026 — https://nato.news-pravda.com/world/2026/05/26/105286.html
- **Confidence: High** (multiple credible sources, confirmed official data)

**2. [Defense Articles] Ukraine ordered 1,500 JDAM-ER guided bombs ($373.6M FMS, approved May 5) — the only confirmed new U.S. defense article transfer this reporting window.**
The Trump administration continues to route Ukraine arms through the Prioritized Ukraine Requirements List (PURL), under which NATO allies purchase and transfer U.S. weapons. No new Presidential Drawdown Authority (PDA) packages have been issued. The FMS sale marks a shift toward commercial arms sales as the primary U.S. transfer mechanism.
- Source: Overt Defense, "Ukraine Orders 1,500 JDAM-ER Guided Bombs from US," May 11, 2026 — https://www.overtdefense.com/2026/05/11/ukraine-orders-1500-jdam-er-guided-bombs-from-us/
- Source: GlobalSecurity.org, "Ukraine – Joint Direct Attack Munitions – Extended Range," May 5, 2026 — https://www.globalsecurity.org/wmd/library/news/ukraine/2026/05/ukraine-260505-state01.htm
- **Confidence: High** (State Department-sourced)

**3. [Funding] A critical House-Senate appropriations gap leaves Ukraine Security Assistance Initiative (USAI) funded by authorization only — no FY2026 appropriation has been enacted.**
House appropriations bill contains $0 for Ukraine; Senate bill contains $800M–$1B. Conference negotiations are unresolved. NDAA FY2026 authorized $400M in USAI but authorization is not appropriation. Trump administration has not requested Ukraine security assistance funds for FY2026.
- Source: OSW Centre for Eastern Studies, "US Defence Budget for 2026: Congress Approves Continued Support for Ukraine," Dec. 19, 2025 — https://www.osw.waw.pl/en/publikacje/analyses/2025-12-19/us-defence-budget-2026-congress-approves-continued-support-ukraine
- Source: The Hill, "Senate Panel Advances Funding Bill with $1 Billion for Ukraine" — https://thehill.com/policy/international/5429889-senate-ukraine-aid-defense-funding/
- **Confidence: High** (Congress.gov and credible reporting)

**4. [Operation Epic Fury / Ukraine Linkage] Operation Epic Fury (U.S.–Israel vs. Iran) has concluded under a ceasefire, but its dual-theater munitions drain is the defining strategic constraint of 2026.**
Operation Epic Fury commenced February 28, 2026; an indefinite ceasefire is now in place as of early May. Iran retains 70% of its pre-war ballistic missile arsenal; underground facilities largely survived. The operation created a munitions competition directly affecting Ukraine's air defense supply chain, with Patriot interceptors most acutely affected.
- Source: White House, "Peace Through Strength: Operation Epic Fury Crushes Iranian Threat as Ceasefire Takes Hold," April 2026 — https://www.whitehouse.gov/releases/2026/04/peace-through-strength-operation-epic-fury-crushes-iranian-threat-as-ceasefire-takes-hold/
- Source: Al Jazeera, "Operation Epic Fury Has Ended: Is the Iran War Over?" May 6, 2026 — https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/5/6/operation-epic-fury-has-ended-is-the-iran-war-over
- **Confidence: High** (official U.S. government and multiple credible outlets)

**5. [Escalation] Russia conducted large-scale nuclear exercises May 19–21 and amplified nuclear rhetoric toward NATO — assessed by ISW as information warfare, not imminent nuclear intent.**
Exercises included Yars ICBM launch, Zircon hypersonic missile, and Sineva submarine-launched ballistic missile. Deputy FM Ryabkov warned of potential NATO-Russia direct clash. DIA assessment remains: Russia unlikely to use nuclear weapons absent existential threat to regime.
- Source: YourNews / ISW assessment, "Russia Signals Nuclear Readiness as NATO Tensions Intensify," May 21, 2026 — https://yournews.com/2026/05/21/6992979/russia-signals-nuclear-readiness-as-nato-tensions-intensify-over-ukraine/
