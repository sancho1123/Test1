## B. TOP ISSUES FOR OUSW COMPTROLLER P/B ANALYSTS

**[Defense Articles / Stockpiles]** Patriot interceptor inventory is critically strained. Epic Fury consumed ~50% of U.S. Patriot stocks. Ukraine already received only 600 interceptors over four years vs. a stated need of 2,000/year. Production lead time for PAC-3 MSE is 42 months. The $3.7B GEM-T contract delivers nothing until 2028. **Likely congressional question:** "Are Patriot interceptor stockpiles adequate for Ukraine, Middle East, and Indo-Pacific requirements simultaneously?"

**[Replenishment / DIB]** U.S. Army 155mm artillery production remains stuck at 40,000 rounds/month — same as September 2024 — against a 100,000/month target that was supposed to be met by October 2024. Multi-theater demand (Ukraine + Epic Fury aftermath + Indo-Pacific readiness) simultaneously pressures every critical munitions category. **Likely congressional question:** "Why hasn't 155mm production hit its stated goal and what is the replenishment timeline?"

**[USAI / Appropriations]** FY2026 NDAA authorized $400M USAI. House appropriations bill provides **$0** USAI (no Trump administration request; committee effort to add funding failed). Senate bill provides **$800M**. Conference not yet resolved. Trump administration is providing only previously committed assistance — no new PDA announced since January 2025. **Likely congressional question:** "What is the status of USAI FY2026 and is Ukraine receiving any new U.S. security assistance?"

**[Epic Fury / Iran Linkage — Confirmed]** Operation Epic Fury directly competed with Ukraine for Patriot interceptors and is documented as the primary reason Ukraine was kept "at the back of the line" for missiles. This is now a confirmed linkage, sourced by multiple major credible outlets. **Likely congressional question:** "Are Epic Fury's munitions demands now resolved, and are any interceptors being redirected to Ukraine?"

**[Nuclear / Escalation — Congressional RFI]** Russia's May 19–21 nuclear exercises are the largest and most public nuclear signaling event of the current conflict. NATO issued explicit deterrence responses. Combined with Russia-Belarus nuclear exercises, this represents a significant escalation signal. **Likely congressional question:** "How does Russia's nuclear signaling affect U.S. force posture, readiness, and extended deterrence commitments?"

**[North Korea / Sanctions]** North Korea supplied approximately 40% of Russia's artillery shells from August 2023 to May 2025. North Korea opened a public war memorial in April 2026 commemorating 2,000+ troops killed fighting for Russia. Russia is planning a 2027–2031 military cooperation agreement with Pyongyang. **Likely congressional question:** "Are sanctions effectively limiting Russia's access to North Korean munitions, and what is the status of Russia-North Korea military cooperation?"

**[Peace Talks / Execution]** Negotiations remain stalled. No near-term ceasefire is likely based on available reporting. This means continued pressure on U.S. aid accounts, no reduction in Ukraine assistance requirements, and continued congressional oversight pressure. **Likely congressional question:** "What is the realistic timeline for a Ukraine-Russia ceasefire and how does that affect our aid obligations?"

### A. U.S.-Provided Defense Articles

- Ukraine requires approximately **2,000 Patriot interceptors per year**; received only **600 over the entire four-year war period** (averaging ~150/year).
- The U.S. began the Epic Fury operation with an estimated **~2,330 Patriot missiles** total inventory.

- Air-defense inventories now stretched across: U.S. forces in Middle East, Indo-Pacific requirements, European/NATO commitments, and Ukraine — in that approximate priority order.

## 11. ANALYST ASSESSMENT

### What Matters Most Today

The convergence of two developments defines today's threat landscape for OUSW Comptroller P/B analysts:

1. **Russia's May 19–21 nuclear exercises** are the most extensive nuclear signaling event since the full-scale invasion began. The exercises are most likely intended as deterrence signaling — not imminent use — but they are unprecedented in scale and cannot be dismissed. NATO's explicit, public deterrence warning in response raises the stakes of any miscalculation.

2. **The Patriot interceptor stockpile crisis** is a documented, confirmed, multi-theater readiness problem. With Epic Fury consuming roughly half the U.S. Patriot inventory and a production lead time of 42 months for the newest variants, there is no near-term solution. Ukraine remains critically underserved relative to its stated requirements. Congress is already asking questions. DoD's acquisition actions ($3.7B GEM-T contract) address the long-term gap but do nothing before 2028.

### Watch Items for Next 7 Days

1. Patriot reallocation decisions post-Epic Fury
2. House-Senate USAI conference resolution ($0 vs. $800M)
3. Russia's next mass strike campaign against Ukrainian cities
4. Iran ceasefire stability and Strait of Hormuz status
5. Any U.S.-facilitated peace talk resumption
