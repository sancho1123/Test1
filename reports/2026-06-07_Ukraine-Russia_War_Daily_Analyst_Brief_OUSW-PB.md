# PART II — ANALYST ANNEX

## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

As of June 7, 2026, the Ukraine–Russia conflict is characterized by Russian air superiority pressure through mass missile/drone strikes and slow but continuing ground advances in Donbas, against a Ukrainian defense that is increasingly strained in its highest-end air defense capabilities.

Russia launched **73 missiles and 656 drones** in its June 2, 2026 mass attack on Ukraine — its largest in over a year — exploiting Ukrainian air defense shortfalls and killing 22 people. Ukraine intercepted 40 of 73 missiles and 602 of 656 drones, indicating a measurable degradation from historical ~90%+ missile intercept rates to 82%.

Ukraine struck St. Petersburg on June 3 with drones (day before Russia's premier economic forum), demonstrating continued deep-strike reach.

On the ground, Russia continues incremental advances in the Kostiantynivka direction, Pokrovsk axis, and near Kupiansk, while Ukraine maintains defensive positions.

The defense industrial and financial picture is critical for P/B analysts: the $3.7B Raytheon Patriot GEM-T contract (April 14, 2026) is the largest single Ukraine-related defense contract of the year but provides no near-term inventory relief. The EU's €90B financial support package and G7 ERA loans backed by frozen Russian assets represent the primary non-U.S. financial lifeline.

### 3.2 THAAD

**System:** Terminal High Altitude Area Defense (THAAD)
- **Transfer Status:** No THAAD transferred to Ukraine. EUCOM-area THAAD batteries redeployed to CENTCOM for Epic Fury.
- **Depletion:** ~198 interceptors fired in first 16 days of Epic Fury (≈40% of entire pre-conflict global inventory of ~534 rounds). By campaign end: **~50% of global inventory expended.**
- **Replenishment Gap:** No new THAAD interceptors delivered to U.S. inventory since July 2023. Next expected delivery: **~April 2027.** Two-year+ zero-delivery window is historically unprecedented.
- **Production Expansion:** Lockheed Martin producing ~96/year; framework agreement (Jan. 29, 2026) for scale-up to 400/year — limited by gallium/germanium availability (China controls ~80% of global supply).
- **Congressional Interest:** CRITICAL
- **Appropriations Implication:** Emergency supplemental needed; no current FY2026 line item for accelerated replenishment.
- **Sources:** [Military Times Race of Attrition](https://www.militarytimes.com/news/your-military/2026/03/06/race-of-attrition-us-militarys-finite-interceptor-stockpile-is-being-tested/) | [Defence Security Asia / RUSI](https://defencesecurityasia.com/en/israel-arrow3-thaad-shortage-iran-war-rusi-interceptor-crisis-2026/) | [Small Wars Journal](https://smallwarsjournal.com/2026/03/27/magazine-depth-iran-missiles-stockpile-readiness/)

### 4.2 THAAD (Lockheed Martin)

- **Current production:** ~96 interceptors/year

- **Critical Mineral Risk:** Gallium and germanium required for THAAD seekers/electronics. China controls ~80%+ of global supply; export restrictions in effect since mid-2023. This is a **single-point-of-failure** for the entire THAAD replenishment program.

Simultaneous demand signals from Ukraine, Epic Fury replenishment, Taiwan contingency pre-positioning, and NATO Article 5 deterrence are competing for the same production lines. CSIS analysis: rebuilding full depleted inventories (Patriot, THAAD, JASSM, Tomahawk, AIM-120) requires **minimum 3 years**, likely longer for THAAD.
