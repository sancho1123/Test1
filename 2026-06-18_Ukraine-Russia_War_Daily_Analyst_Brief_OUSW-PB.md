## B. TOP ISSUES FOR OUSW COMPTROLLER P/B ANALYSTS

1. **[Stockpiles | Replenishment | Readiness]** The Iran war exhausted an estimated **50–61% of U.S. Patriot PAC-3 interceptors** and ~25% of the THAAD stockpile in 39 days. Ukraine received sharply reduced monthly delivery quotas as a direct result. The FY2027 DoW budget requests $70B for munitions replenishment (a $47B increase), including $22B specifically for Iran war-related munitions. **Congressional question:** Is the U.S. able to simultaneously replenish Iran war losses AND meet Ukraine's interceptor needs?  
   Source: Fox News/CSIS, "US used roughly half its Patriot interceptors in Iran war," 2026 — https://www.foxnews.com/politics/us-drains-critical-missile-stockpiles-iran-war-yearslong-rebuild-looms

2. **[Funding | USAI | Congressional RFI]** The House-passed Ukraine Support Act ($8B military finance loans, USAI through FY2027) is stuck in the Senate and opposed by the White House. FY2026 USAI stands at only **$400M** — a dramatic reduction from the April 2024 supplemental's ~$14B. Analysts should be ready to explain the USAI funding gap and whether current authority levels are sufficient.  
   Source: TWZ, "Shift In Ukraine Support in Congress's Proposed Pentagon Budget" — https://www.twz.com/news-features/shift-in-ukraine-support-in-congresss-proposed-pentagon-budget

3. **[Defense Articles | Readiness | Multi-Theater]** Pentagon redirected 20,000 anti-drone interceptors earmarked for Ukraine under USAI to Israel and U.S. Middle East forces in June 2025. Congress responded in the NDAA to limit future diversion. As of June 2026, whether sufficient replacement units have been provided to Ukraine remains an active oversight issue.  
   Source: OSW Centre for Eastern Studies, "US defence budget for 2026," Dec 19, 2025 — https://www.osw.waw.pl/en/publikacje/analyses/2025-12-19/us-defence-budget-2026-congress-approves-continued-support-ukraine

4. **[DIB | Replenishment | Readiness]** 155mm shell production is ~40,000/month vs. a 100,000/month target repeatedly delayed, now projected for mid-2026. Russia produces ~250,000 rounds/month. A new Iowa Army Ammunition Plant won't open until 2029. A Camden, AR facility can handle up to 50,000 rounds/month when fully operational. The artillery shell gap is a known readiness risk.  
   Source: National Defense Magazine, "Army Falls Short of 155mm Production Goal," Aug 2025 — https://www.nationaldefensemagazine.org/articles/2025/8/14/army-falls-short-of-155mm-production-goal; Federal News Network — https://federalnewsnetwork.com/defense-main/2026/03/billions-are-flowing-into-munitions-but-there-are-limits-to-how-quickly-the-u-s-can-replenish-its-stockpiles/

5. **[Epic Fury / Iran Linkage | Readiness]** Geneva signing ceremony June 19 formalizes the US–Iran ceasefire. Post-Epic Fury, Iran reportedly reconstitutes drone production capacity faster than Western intelligence can calibrate. Russia–Iran drone/missile cooperation continues even as Iran regroups. The post-Epic Fury strategic environment continues to compete with Ukraine for limited U.S. and allied air defense assets.  
   Source: militaryspend.org — https://militaryspend.org/operation-epic-fury; Hudson Institute, "Operation Epic Fury Situation Report" — https://www.hudson.org/missile-defense/operation-epic-fury-situation-report-battlefield-effects-strategic-outcomes-can-kasapoglu

Chinese firms now account for ~80% of electronic components in Russian drones. The scale of Chinese support—estimated at $4B in dual-use goods in 2024—is a major congressional oversight question.  
   Source: Fortune, "China expanding aid for Russia's war," Feb 14, 2026 — https://fortune.com/2026/02/14/china-aid-drones-minerals-russia-war-ukraine-invasion-putin-xi-jinping/

7. **[Escalation Risk | Congressional RFI]** Russia struck a nuclear fuel storage facility near Chornobyl on June 7 using a Shahed drone, damaging a fuel-reception building. This represents a significant escalation threshold and is likely to generate congressional questions.
