6. Operation Epic Fury / Iran — Ukraine–Russia War Linkage

Confirmed Linkage — Direct Stockpile Competition

Operation Epic Fury (U.S.-Israel vs. Iran, Feb 28 – Apr 7, 2026) and the Ukraine-Russia war directly competed for the same U.S. and allied air defense interceptor inventories. This competition is the primary driver of the current Ukraine interceptor crisis. This linkage is confirmed by multiple credible independent sources including CSIS, CNN, and Asia Times.

A. Key Epic Fury Facts

  • Operation dates: Feb 28 – Apr 7, 2026 (40 days). U.S. code name: Operation Epic Fury. Israeli code name: Operation Roaring Lion. Stated objectives: destroy Iran nuclear program, IRGC leadership, ballistic missile infrastructure, naval capabilities, proxy networks. Source: White House, Mar 2026 · DoW Fact Sheet, Mar 23, 2026
  • Opening strike: Killed Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei; triggered Operation True Promise IV retaliation across 7 countries (Bahrain, Jordan, Kuwait, Qatar, Saudi Arabia, UAE, Iraq). Source: Quwa, 2026
  • Ceasefire: April 7, 2026. Iran agreed to ceasefire and reopened Strait of Hormuz. Trump administration announced agreement. Source: White House, Apr 2026

B. Weapons and Stockpile Competition — Direct Ukraine Impact

Both conflicts drew from the same U.S. Patriot, THAAD, Tomahawk, and JASSM inventories. The result: Ukraine's air defense interceptor resupply was curtailed mid-conflict, creating the current critical shortage described in Section 3. GCC Patriot inventories (a key allied resupply source for Ukraine) were depleted ~86% during Epic Fury. Rebuild timeline 3–5 years with no THAAD deliveries to U.S. inventory in 2026.
