SecDef Hegseth released funds in late April only after Sen. McConnell's Washington Post op-ed. As of May 2026, the Senate Armed Services Committee reports the Pentagon has not provided Congress a detailed spending plan for these funds.

  Source: Stars and Stripes, "Senators question Pentagon's lack of Ukraine spending plan," May 19, 2026, https://www.stripes.com/branches/army/2026-05-19/congress-army-budget-no-details-21720476.html
  Confidence: **High**

- **[Appropriations]** The House FY26 defense appropriations bill contains zero USAI funding; the Senate bill contains $800M. A conference resolution has not yet been reached — the gap in appropriated (vs. authorized) funding for Ukraine remains a live budget execution risk.
  **Congressional question:** "What is the status of the FY26 defense appropriations bill Ukraine provision, and what happens to USAI authorities if full-year appropriations are not enacted?"
  Source: OSW Centre for Eastern Studies, "US defence budget for 2026," Dec. 19, 2025, https://www.osw.waw.pl/en/publikacje/analyses/2025-12-19/us-defence-budget-2026-congress-approves-continued-support-ukraine

These systems are also earmarked for Indo-Pacific deterrence. Ukraine's civilian air defense shortfall is a direct downstream effect — PAC-3 MSE production is ~600/year vs. wartime consumption of ~225/day during Epic Fury peak.

### 3. DEFENSE ARTICLES, WEAPONS, STOCKPILES, AND MUNITIONS *(Chunk 4 of 5)*

#### A. US-Provided Defense Articles

**Patriot Air Defense:** Ukraine is in a critical Patriot interceptor shortage. Zelenskyy confirmed an agreement with partners on new Patriot systems and missiles (June 3), but funding sources remain unclear. PAC-3 MSE global production was ~620 interceptors in 2025 (Lockheed Martin) vs. wartime consumption of ~225/day during Operation Epic Fury. The shortage is exacerbated by Epic Fury drawdowns. Lockheed is moving to triple production capacity but 2,000/year is not achievable before 2030. Single-point bottleneck: Boeing seeker assembly (Huntsville, AL, 650–700/year capacity).
Sources: Defense Post, https://thedefensepost.com/2026/01/07/pac-3-mse-triple-production/; FPRI, https://www.fpri.org/article/2026/05/scaling-patriot-production-the-industrial-base-crisis-explained/

**HIMARS/M270:** Ukraine has 39 HIMARS and 30 M270 systems; 4 confirmed destroyed, 4 damaged. Systems remain operational and critical to Ukrainian counter-battery and interdiction fires.
Source: Ukraine War Analytics, https://ukraine-war-analytics.com/analysis/ukraine-military-aid-total-2026.html

**ATACMS:** Ukraine fired ATACMS on February 24, 2026, against a Russian army command node in Donetsk Oblast. US authorized transfer of ATACMS from Germany. 100+ ATACMS reportedly transferred. ATACMS global inventory significantly degraded by Epic Fury (~46% of combined ATACMS/PrSM consumed in first 16 days).
Sources: UNITED24 Media, https://united24media.com/latest-news/us-approves-transfer-of-atacms-and-patriot-missiles-from-germany-to-ukraine-8277; Small Wars Journal, https://smallwarsjournal.com/2026/03/27/magazine-depth-iran-missiles-stockpile-readiness/

**155mm Artillery:** US producing ~56,000 rounds/month; target is 100,000 (not yet achieved). Army dissatisfied with Mesquite, TX facility performance. Ukraine's consumption rate can exceed US monthly production in days during intensive operations.
Source: Breaking Defense, https://breakingdefense.com/2026/02/army-official-not-happy-with-gds-handling-of-155mm-contract-as-production-lags/

**F-16 Support:** Ukraine has operated F-16s in combat for over a year. No specific new F-16 transfers announced in this reporting window.

**NASAMS, Javelins, Bradleys, Strykers, Abrams:** No new confirmed transfer announcements in this 24–48 hour window. Javelin and Stinger inventories remain subject to replenishment pressures across theaters.

#### C. Stockpile and Readiness Issues

- **Patriot PAC-3 MSE:** Critical global shortage. Production ~620/year (2025). Target: 2,000/year by 2030. Boeing seeker bottleneck (single facility, Huntsville, AL). Epic Fury consumed ~40% of US-operated THAAD and large numbers of PAC-3 MSE. Ukraine is effectively at the back of the global queue. Confidence: **High**.
- **ATACMS/PrSM:** ~46% of combined inventory consumed in first 16 days of Epic Fury. Restoration timeline: 3–4 years for full reconstitution. Confidence: **High**.
- **THAAD Interceptors:** ~40% of US inventory consumed in first 16 days of Epic Fury. Ukraine does not operate THAAD, but US ability to transfer escalatory alternatives is constrained. Confidence: **High**.
- **155mm Artillery Shells:** US at ~56,000/month vs. 100,000 target. Ukraine consumption can exceed monthly US production within days during peak operations. Confidence: **High**.
- **Shahed/Geran-2 Threat:** Russia aiming for 1,000 Geran-2 drones/day production. Ukraine must intercept with expensive interceptors. Counter-UAS demand creates further pressure. Confidence: **Medium** (Ukrainian intelligence assessment).

### 6. OPERATION EPIC FURY / IRAN — UKRAINE LINKAGE *(Chunk 5 of 5)*

#### A. Weapons/Stockpile Competition
**Confirmed evidence.** Both conflicts are directly drawing from the same US and allied missile defense inventories. Operation Epic Fury (Feb 28–Apr 2026) consumed approximately 40% of US THAAD interceptors and ~46% of ATACMS/PrSM in just 16 days of operations. Patriot PAC-3 MSE was also heavily consumed. Ukraine is in a "critical state of vulnerability" due to Patriot interceptor shortage — directly attributed in part to Epic Fury depletion. Global PAC-3 MSE annual production of ~620 missiles (2025) cannot simultaneously meet CENTCOM wartime consumption rates, US readiness reserves, Indo-Pacific deterrence needs, and Ukraine deliveries. The June 1–2 Russian strikes killed 22 Ukrainian civilians, with reporting directly linking insufficient interceptor stocks (partly from Epic Fury) to the inability to stop all incoming threats.

Are both conflicts drawing from the same inventory? **Yes — confirmed.**
Is one conflict affecting the other's supply priority? **Yes — Epic Fury consumed first; Ukraine gets less.**
Are replenishment timelines affected? **Yes — 2–4 years to reconstitute key inventories.**
Congressional concern? **High — both SASC and HAC have raised this.**

Sources: CSIS, https://www.csis.org/analysis/last-rounds-status-key-munitions-iran-war-ceasefire; The Guardian/Sweden Pravda, https://sweden.news-pravda.com/en/world/2026/06/03/13378.html

#### B. Drone/Missile Warfare Linkage
**Confirmed evidence.** Iran supplied Russia with Shahed-136 drones and technical assistance since 2022. Russia produces its Geran-2 variant at a factory in Tatarstan, targeting 1,000/day production. Iran's drone and ballistic missile technology has been simultaneously used in Ukraine (via Russia) and against US/allied forces in the Middle East during Epic Fury. North Korea supplied KN-23 and KN-24 ballistic missiles to Russia — the same missile design families that threatened US forces during Iranian proxy attacks.
Sources: Asia Times, https://asiatimes.com/2026/02/irans-drones-giving-russia-a-critical-edge-in-ukraine-war/; CSIS/JINSA, https://jinsa.org/jinsa_report/the-axis-behind-iran-how-china-russia-and-north-korea-sustain-tehrans-military-threat/

#### C. Russia-Iran Relationship
**Confirmed evidence with caveat.** Russia and Iran signed a cooperation treaty in January 2025. Operation Epic Fury killed Supreme Leader Khamenei and significantly disrupted Iranian military capacity. Russia's ability to receive new weapons shipments from Iran is constrained by the ongoing US-Iran military confrontation and ceasefire. However, pre-war deliveries of drones and missiles are already inside Russia. North Korea is reportedly filling any gap in Iran's weapons provision to Russia.
Sources: Military.com, https://www.military.com/feature/2026/03/24/north-koreas-rogue-strategic-pipeline-arming-irans-war-effort.html; CSIS, https://www.csis.org/analysis/what-do-strikes-iran-mean-china-russia-and-north-korea

#### D. Energy/Economic Effects
**Confirmed evidence.** The 2026 Strait of Hormuz closure (March 2026) removed 10M+ barrels/day from global markets; Brent crude exceeded $120/barrel at peak. Oil has since fallen ~20% from peak on ceasefire optimism. Russia's oil and gas revenues are now only ~22% of the federal budget; defense spending is ~38% of total federal expenditure. Lower global oil prices (from anticipated Hormuz reopening) reduce Russia's war-financing capacity. The fragile US-Iran ceasefire is the primary determinant of near-term energy price trajectory.
Sources: CNBC, https://www.cnbc.com/2026/05/29/oil-prices-iran-ceasefire-us-trump-strait-hormuz-energy-costs.html; Moscow Times, https://www.themoscowtimes.com/2026/01/02/russias-economy-in-2026-more-war-slower-growth-and-higher-taxes-a91579

#### E. Strategic Bandwidth and Force Allocation
**Analyst judgment based on credible reporting.** CENTCOM's prosecution of Epic Fury has diverted significant US strategic bandwidth from EUCOM. The same THAAD batteries, Patriot systems, and PrSM missiles that underpin European and Ukrainian deterrence were consumed in CENTCOM AOR. The Senate has pressed the Pentagon for details on force posture changes in Europe and Ukraine spending plans — indicating congressional awareness of the CENTCOM-EUCOM tension. Ukraine's congressional funding base may be eroded by US public focus on Iran/Hormuz and fatigue from both conflicts simultaneously.
Sources: DefenseScoop, https://defensescoop.com/2026/03/12/nato-rewriting-integrated-air-defense-plans-gen-grynkewich/; Stars and Stripes, https://www.stripes.com/branches/army/2026-05-19/congress-army-budget-no-details-21720476.html
